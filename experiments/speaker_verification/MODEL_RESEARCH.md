# Contemporary speaker verification models for Dogwalk

Research cut-off: **2026-11-10**. Live primary repositories, model cards, artifact metadata, and papers checked on that date. Research only: no model execution, GPU use, production changes, or commits. “Available” below means a public artifact is listed by the publisher, not that I downloaded and successfully executed it.

## Bottom line

**Yes, credible newer replacements exist, with material improvements on published speaker-verification benchmarks. No, those results do not establish a materially better passive PSTN authenticator for Adam.** Keep SpeechBrain ECAPA as a baseline, compare **ReDimNet2-B3/B6 and CAM++ next**, then test **ERes2NetV2** for short speech. ReDimNet2+ is an unusually relevant codec-robustness experiment, but lower-confidence evidence, not a proven winner.

This crosses three disciplines: speaker representation learning, statistical decision/calibration, and authentication security. Better embeddings do not by themselves solve the latter two. Dogwalk must preserve ordinary-telephone access, including borrowed phones, with no visual prerequisite or spoken password/challenge. Natural conversation can accumulate evidence; uncertainty must not silently grant permissions or falsely claim strong authentication.

The [SpeechBrain card](https://huggingface.co/speechbrain/spkrec-ecapa-voxceleb) explicitly dates its release `05-03-21`, cites the 2020 architecture, reports **0.80% EER on cleaned VoxCeleb1-test**, expects 16 kHz mono, and disclaims performance on other datasets. Its age alone is not grounds for replacement. Training data, input handling, scoring, and the deployed channel can matter more than architecture age.

## Runnable open-weight shortlist

Numbers below are **publisher-reported**, not local measurements. They are not a unified leaderboard: trial lists, duration, training data, score normalization, and calibration differ. VoxCeleb consists of real-world internet interview/video speech, not pristine studio speech, but its cleaned trial protocols are still **not 8 kHz PSTN evaluations**.

### 1. ReDimNet2: first contemporary accuracy/compute comparison

- **Released, not just a paper:** [official PalabraAI repository](https://github.com/PalabraAI/redimnet2), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11841), and [release assets](https://github.com/PalabraAI/redimnet2/releases/tag/v1.0.0). [GitHub release API](https://api.github.com/repos/PalabraAI/redimnet2/releases) reports v1.0.0 published **2026-03-04**, with Vox2 B0–B6 checkpoint assets that day; VoxBlink2+Vox2 B6 assets **2026-04-23**; CN-Celeb mixture B3/B6 assets **2026-07-06**. These are all before this review date. The 2026 paper is not a future announcement.
- **Exact candidates:** `b3-vox2-lm.pt` and `b6-vox2-lm.pt`, then `b6-vb2+vox2_v0-lm.pt` if extra-data training helps. Official `torch.hub` loading supports CPU PyTorch and 16 kHz waveforms. Pin source revision and checkpoint, do not execute a moving Hub repository blindly.
- **Scale:** B3 **4.1M parameters**, reported **2.70 GMACs**; B6 **12.3M**, **13.05 GMACs** in the authors' setup. Artifact sizes about **17.9 MB / 50.9 MB** for Vox2 LM. GMACs are not milliseconds or Mac latency.
- **Evidence:** Vox2-trained B3 reports Vox1-O/E/H EER **0.42/0.66/1.22%**; B6 **0.29/0.52/0.99%**. B6 VoxBlink2+Vox2 reports **0.23/0.35/0.67%**, plus SITW **0.74%**, VOICES **2.55%**, CN-Celeb **7.49%**. These additional domains strengthen the case beyond VoxCeleb, but are not a telephone benchmark. The CN-Celeb-trained mixture improves CN-Celeb and slightly worsens VoxCeleb, a useful reminder that newer/more data is not universally better.
- **Short speech / PSTN:** no matched 8 kHz PSTN or Dogwalk-length accuracy evidence established from the inspected release. Compare both LM and pretraining variants if 1–3 seconds is important; long-utterance fine-tuning need not help the shortest windows.
- **License:** official repository states **MIT**. Retain checkpoint provenance and inspect distribution/data obligations separately. WeSpeaker also ships a [2026 ReDimNet2-B6 checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/Wespeaker/wespeaker-voxceleb-redimnet2-B6-LM), but that is a separate implementation/training artifact, not interchangeable with the official weights.

### 2. CAM++: low-cost operational challenger, not newest architecture

- [2023 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00332), [3D-Speaker implementation](https://github.com/modelscope/3D-Speaker), [WeSpeaker checkpoint and ONNX](https://huggingface.co/Wespeaker/wespeaker-voxceleb-campplus). HF repository metadata dates this particular release to **2024-05-06**. This is checkpoint publication, not a new 2024 architecture.
- **Candidate:** WeSpeaker VoxCeleb CAM++, compare non-LM and [LM](https://huggingface.co/Wespeaker/wespeaker-voxceleb-campplus-LM). Avoid silently substituting the separate Mandarin 200k-speaker ModelScope checkpoint and attributing VoxCeleb recipe numbers to it.
- **Scale:** WeSpeaker reports **7.18M parameters / 1.15G FLOPs** in its recipe. Released ONNX makes CPU deployment attractive. The paper specifically targets better efficiency than ECAPA, but no measured M3 Max speedup is claimed here.
- **Evidence:** [same WeSpeaker recipe](https://github.com/wenet-e2e/wespeaker/blob/master/examples/voxceleb/v2/README.md) reports CAM++ **0.803%** Vox1-O-clean with plain scoring; **0.707%** with LM, **0.659%** with LM+AS-Norm. Its ECAPA1024 counterpart is **0.856/0.798/0.728%**, respectively. A more modest matched-recipe improvement than selectively comparing unrelated best numbers. The 3D-Speaker CAM++ result of **0.65%** comes from another recipe.
- **Short speech / PSTN:** no genuine 8 kHz PSTN evidence established for this released checkpoint. It is a strong efficiency comparator, not a certified telephone model.
- **License:** WeSpeaker code and HF card say **Apache-2.0**; [WeSpeaker's pretrained-model policy](https://github.com/wenet-e2e/wespeaker/blob/master/docs/pretrained.md) separately says weights follow dataset licenses and cites VoxCeleb **CC BY 4.0**. Record this discrepancy rather than treating the code license as blanket clearance for weights.

### 3. ERes2NetV2: best-targeted published short-duration experiment

- [2024 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02167v1), [official toolkit](https://github.com/modelscope/3D-Speaker), [released checkpoint](https://modelscope.cn/models/iic/speech_eres2netv2_sv_zh-cn_16k-common). Toolkit news records 2024 release activity. [ModelScope artifact metadata](https://modelscope.cn/api/v1/models/iic/speech_eres2netv2_sv_zh-cn_16k-common/repo/files?Revision=master&Recursive=true) lists `pretrained_eres2netv2.ckpt`, **71,768,231 bytes**, committed in April 2024. It is not merely a training recipe.
- **Scale:** paper **17.8M parameters / 12.6G FLOPs**. PyTorch inference via `speakerlab/bin/infer_sv.py`; toolkit also has an ONNX Runtime path. CPU feasibility is plausible, not benchmarked here.
- **Short-duration evidence:** paper uses **full-length enrollment**, test crops of **3 seconds / 2 seconds**. Vox1-O EER **0.98/1.48%** versus its reproduced ECAPA **1.27/1.95%**. Full-length result **0.61%**. This is meaningful evidence for the architecture, not evidence for a 1-second “yes,” short enrollment, or telephone speech.
- **Important checkpoint distinction:** the readily released `zh-cn_16k-common` card says it was trained on **about 200k Mandarin speakers**, not the paper's VoxCeleb training set. Its card reports **3.81% CN-Celeb** versus CAM++ **4.32%**, both about 200k training speakers; the card's ECAPA **7.45%** uses only about 3k, so that comparison does not isolate architecture. Do not assign the paper's 0.61% to this checkpoint.
- **PSTN:** explicitly 16 kHz input, no real PSTN evaluation found in these inspected sources. Mandarin-to-Adam-English transfer remains an experiment.
- **License:** checkpoint card explicitly **Apache License 2.0**, as is toolkit code.

### 4. ECAPA2: direct successor with very-short-speech analysis

- [ASRU 2023 architecture, January 2024 arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08342v1), [official model](https://huggingface.co/Jenthe/ECAPA2). [HF metadata](https://huggingface.co/api/models/Jenthe/ECAPA2) gives repository creation **2023-08-03**, latest modification **2024-05-16**. These metadata dates do not prove a new May 2024 weight training run.
- **Released artifact:** `ecapa2.pt`, about **108.9 MB**, **27M parameters**, TorchScript loaded with `torch.jit.load(..., map_location='cpu')`; 16 kHz waveform input. Straightforward CPU research candidate. Card warns initial JIT optimization may be very slow; distinguish cold start from steady state.
- **Evidence:** paper reports Vox1-O/E/H **0.34/0.52/0.99%** with its score-normalization/calibration protocol. Custom Vox1-S trials, random **0.5–2 second test crops**, yield **7.92% EER** versus ECAPA **11.05%**, with no normalization/calibration on that custom set. These much higher errors are directly relevant to avoiding overconfidence in tiny utterances. Do not compare this 7.92% with ERes2NetV2's fixed 2-second result as if trial conditions matched.
- **PSTN:** input masking and short/overlapping speech robustness are studied, but no matched real telephone test found here.
- **License:** **CC BY-NC 4.0**, not Apache or unrestricted commercial weights. Appropriate as a research comparator if the noncommercial condition fits; do not silently promote to a commercial deployment.

### 5. ReDimNet2+: codec-targeted, promising but provisional

- [Publisher model card and weights](https://huggingface.co/lab260/redimnet2-plus), [code](https://github.com/lab260ru/redimnet2-plus). [HF metadata](https://huggingface.co/api/models/lab260/redimnet2-plus) dates repository creation **2026-06-14**, last modification **2026-09-11**; [file listing](https://huggingface.co/api/models/lab260/redimnet2-plus/tree/main) confirms **100,531,990-byte** `pytorch_model_fsdp.bin`. Public weights exist before this review date.
- **Do not misdate the evidence:** “submitted to ICASSP 2027” is a current submission claim, **not a 2027 accepted result**. The repository says the full paper will be linked when available; the inspected evidence is the authors' card/README/code, not an independently reviewed paper.
- **Same B6 architecture**, **12.3M parameters**: newer adaptation/data/schedule, not a new backbone. Claims codec augmentation including **A-law, μ-law, AMR-NB, G.723.1 and 8/16 kHz** paths, plus noise/reverb/filter augmentation and multi-corpus training.
- **Evidence:** authors' random **4-second** cosine-scoring protocol reports Vox1-O **0.351%**, pooled O/E/H **0.824%**, and their **26-condition codec/waveform stress EER 1.991%**, versus the chosen public ReDimNet2 baseline **1.601/2.420/7.214%**. The stress score is **not** an 8 kHz-only number or a real PSTN benchmark. The baseline result is far worse than official full-utterance results: exact checkpoint, frontend and scoring reproduction must be audited before crediting the whole difference to adaptation. Their comparison is shared evaluation protocol, **not equal training data**.
- **Integration caution:** card example does not construct the full runnable model and its `load_audio` helper does not actually resample to 16 kHz. Use repository/config-matched inference, verify sample-rate handling and reproduce known trials first. FSDP wrappers and a CUDA-oriented Docker evaluation pipeline add friction; underlying PyTorch inference supports CPU, but Mac compatibility and latency remain untested. Retrieval graph reranking is not needed for one enrolled Adam versus an incoming call.
- **License:** model card **Apache-2.0**. Lower-confidence research slot, particularly relevant after a conventional B3/B6 baseline works.

## Other contemporary directions, not first comparisons

- **VoxBlink2-pretrained SimAM-ResNet34/100:** actual 2024 checkpoints and ONNX exports in the [WeSpeaker model list](https://github.com/wenet-e2e/wespeaker/blob/master/docs/pretrained.md). Its recipe reports **0.415/0.229%** Vox1-O for 34/100 without AS-Norm, **25.2M/50.2M parameters**. Useful if testing training-data breadth, but adds a larger model without telephone-specific evidence. Model/data license needs checking separately.
- **W2V-BERT 2.0 + speaker head:** actual checkpoint/adapter links now exist in that same official list, with [2025 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04213) and 2025–2026 toolkit support. A generic SSL checkpoint alone is not a speaker verifier. The adapted system is a much heavier accuracy-ceiling experiment, not an obvious cost/latency win for one operator. Do not recommend an unadapted WavLM/W2V model as a drop-in verifier.
- **Hosted:** no hosted API is necessary for this first comparison. Enterprise passive voice biometrics may be telephony-oriented but would need actual trial access, pricing, retention terms and an evaluation against the same calls, not vendor “accuracy” percentages. Do not recommend obsolete Azure speaker recognition: [Microsoft's release notes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/releasenotes) explicitly remove it in SDK 1.47 (November 2025) due to retirement. ASR diarization, including named speaker labels local to one recording, is not enrolled-person verification.

## PSTN evidence and deployment cost: what is actually known

All five selected input interfaces expect **16 kHz**. Decode real telephony audio correctly (including 8 kHz μ-law/A-law when that is the source), then resample for the model. **Upsampling does not restore missing upper-band identity cues.** Preserve the original channel path and avoid accidentally interpreting 8 kHz samples as 16 kHz. Test one-sided and both-sided telephone degradation, not just full-bandwidth enrollment against full-bandwidth tests.

Real telephone evidence is a distinct literature. [WeSpeaker's SRE16 recipe](https://github.com/wenet-e2e/wespeaker/blob/master/examples/sre/v2/README.md), linked to the [NIST SRE16 evaluation](https://www.nist.gov/itl/iad/mig/speaker-recognition-evaluation-2016), reports **15.4% pooled EER with cosine, 11.689% PLDA, 5.788% adapted PLDA** for its own ResNet34 recipe. This does **not** rank the shortlisted checkpoints or predict Adam's error rate. It illustrates that telephone-domain adaptation and backend choice can dominate gains from a newer backbone. The root README quotes a different historical aggregate; use the recipe-specific protocol, not an isolated toolkit headline.

No CPU/M3/cloud runtime measurements were performed, and no reliable per-call dollar quote is available from these sources. These are small enough for **CPU-first inference experiments**, not a reason to rent a GPU for a sole operator. Benchmark on Adam's M3 Max and the actual deployment sandbox CPU, rather than assuming MPS/TensorRT acceleration. ONNX CPU is an established deployment path for CAM++ and WeSpeaker models; export/provider parity must still be checked.

Measure warm and cold p50/p95 latency, RSS, thread count and concurrency at **1/2/3/5/10 seconds of voiced audio**, including decode, resample, VAD and features. Weight-file size is not peak RSS, FLOPs from different papers are not necessarily comparable, and neither predicts wall time reliably. Report time-to-enough-speech separately from compute: a 5-second evidence window imposes at least 5 seconds of speech collection, usually more elapsed conversational time, even if extraction takes milliseconds. Cache enrollment embeddings and keep a CPU model warm; incremental overlapping windows save no compute unless implemented intentionally. With a pre-existing sandbox, incremental CPU time is a more meaningful first cost metric than GPU-hour pricing.

## Recommended next experiment, enrollment, and decision policy

1. **Preserve the current ECAPA+Breeze synthetic test as a plumbing/sanity check**, not an authentication evaluation. Add CAM++ and official ReDimNet2 B3/B6 first. Pin model ID, code revision, weight hash, preprocessing, scoring, and license. Reproduce a few known publisher trials before interpreting score changes.
2. **Collect consented real Adam calls on multiple days**, including his usual phone, another/borrowed handset, quiet/noisy settings, and differing speaking styles. Trusted initial enrollment must have independently established provenance; “first caller wins” is not secure enrollment. A practical pilot target is several independent calls and roughly 1–3 minutes of usable enrollment speech in total, not a model-mandated minimum. Keep multiple channel/session templates or a validated normalized centroid. Do not auto-enroll or update templates from merely accepted calls: that invites poisoning.
3. **Add real non-Adam callers**, including reasonably similar voices. Adam-only positives estimate false rejection, not false acceptance. Keep entire calls/days out of calibration; never train a threshold on one chunk and test it on a neighboring chunk from the same recording. Disjoint conversational content also reduces phrase leakage.
4. **Evaluate matched conditions:** full-band original, band-limited 8 kHz, μ-law/A-law encode/decode, then real PSTN captures. Include clean→telephone and telephone→telephone enrollment/test, short speech bins and cumulative voiced duration. Simulated codecs are useful controlled ablations, not replacements for real captures, handset effects, network processing, packet loss and channel mismatch.
5. **Calibrate each model independently.** Cosine is not a probability; neither SpeechBrain's default nor a model-card threshold (such as 0.365 or 0.79) transfers to Dogwalk. Select thresholds on held-out telephone calls for an explicit acceptable false-accept cost, then report false-reject rate at that operating point, DET/ROC, confidence bounds, and time-to-decision. EER describes a trade-off, not an operational security guarantee. With zero false accepts among N independent trials, the approximate 95% upper bound is still 3/N; many correlated crop pairs do not create N independent adversaries.
6. **Use an explicit insufficient-evidence outcome**, rather than “not Adam” on tiny/noisy/overlapped speech. Natural ongoing conversation can collect more speech without a fixed challenge. Longer-term, validate score fusion/calibration with duration and quality, accounting for correlated windows; do not repeatedly take a maximum until one window passes. Evaluate **call-level** false accepts as well as window-level errors. Restrict the pilot's consequences until its threat model is accepted; designing an ordinary-call-compatible recovery/authorization path is separate work, not a reason to introduce a required screen or silently bypass security.

### What the synthetic same-generator test cannot tell us

Breeze voices can establish that distinct synthetic voice identities produce repeatable embeddings, test resampling/codec handling, and catch obvious bugs. They cannot establish real-Adam enrollment fidelity, genuine human within-speaker variability across illness/emotion/handsets/days, realistic human impostor distributions, or resistance to an Adam-targeted clone. A model might exploit stable generator/voice-preset artifacts rather than the features that survive a real call. Same text, seed, speaker-conditioning method and generator introduce confounds; multiple different texts and seeds help the smoke test but do not remove the synthetic-to-human domain gap. Same-generator voices are also not independent evidence about unknown future synthesis attacks.

## Verification is not anti-spoofing

A speaker embedding asks **“does this sound like the enrolled voice?”**, not **“is a live Adam authorizing this?”** Replay and high-quality voice cloning can succeed precisely because identity matching works. Passive spoof detection is a separate countermeasure with its own channel/generalization failures. [ASVspoof 2021](https://www.asvspoof.org/asvspoof2021/asvspoof2021_evaluation_plan.pdf) explicitly separates speaker verification from spoof countermeasures and includes telephony-transmitted logical-access attacks. A generic different-synthetic-speaker comparison is neither a replay test nor a targeted cloning attack test.

For security context, current [NIST SP 800-63B-4 biometric guidance](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/) says **“Biometric comparison based on voice SHALL NOT be used.”** This is a federal assurance guideline, not a universal legal prohibition on Adam's personal experiment. It is strong reason not to represent voice-only passive verification as high-assurance authentication. Separate genuine-impostor evaluation from replay/TTS/voice-conversion attack evaluation, and evaluate any anti-spoof countermeasure through the actual telephone channel. Caller ID is not a substitute for those guarantees.

**Decision:** ECAPA is a defensible baseline, not a contemporary best-model claim. The most informative next comparison is **ECAPA vs CAM++ vs official ReDimNet2 B3/B6 on held-out real telephone calls**, with fixed preprocessing and per-model calibration. Then test ERes2NetV2/ECAPA2 for duration robustness and ReDimNet2+ for codec robustness. Prefer the cheapest model that meets the selected real-call operating point, even if it is the oldest.
