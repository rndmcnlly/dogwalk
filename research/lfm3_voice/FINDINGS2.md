# Round two: LFM3 as separately scored organs

2026-11-12. Plan: `PLAN2.md`. Machine-readable aggregates: `RESULTS2.json`
(regenerated by `summarize2.py` from ignored `artifacts/round2/`).

**Verdict.** Splitting the frontend into organs works for speech, but not for
routing.
- **Verbatim TTS:** Orchestrator scripts rendered verbatim through male `/tts`
  are fast and faithful. This organ is ready for further local work.
- **Router:** a probability-scored LFM router looks excellent on the suite it
  was tuned on. It does not transfer: on fresh held-out turns it missed two
  positives, one of them a critical correction or cancellation, and fired
  false Handoffs on 10–11 of 15 negatives.
- **Orchestrator-always baseline:** a strong Orchestrator stand-in took no
  operation on any of 24 negative Handoffs (main and held-out) and handled
  every positive correctly, including the critical items the router missed. That makes the
  frontend router an optimization rather than a safety boundary.
- **Status:** LFM remains unready as a semantic controller. It is a plausible
  listener (ASR) and speaker (TTS) behind an Orchestrator that sees every
  caller turn.

Same runtime, model revision (Q8_0), serving build and frozen caller WAVs as
round one (`runtime.md`). The suite is synthetic. One M3 Max, no human
listening, no PSTN.

**instruction-only**: the files were readable on disk and agents were told not
to open them.

## A. Router organ

**Token probabilities with audio input are native.** `/v1/chat/completions`
with an `input_audio` part, `max_tokens: 1`, `logprobs` and `top_logprobs: 20`
returned first-token alternatives. A decision takes about 160 ms with native
audio and about 110 ms with text. No sampling fallback was needed.

**Setup.**
- The router sees a host snapshot: pending Handoffs, active instructions, known
  facts and the last four ledger lines. Caller lines in the snapshot are what
  the host observed through LFM STT.
- It then sees the current caller turn in one of three arms: native audio
  (16 kHz PCM16), oracle text, or LFM STT text.
- Snapshots use gold prior routing, so the organ is scored on the current turn
  only.
- The router is consulted only on caller turns (25 of 40 points: 16 positive,
  9 negative). The other 15 negatives are briefs, results, pending notices and
  commentary. The host never asks the router about those; that zero is host
  behavior.
- Exactly three framings were tried, all on the main suite. The selection rule
  was fixed before results: fewest false Handoffs at 16/16 recall summed over
  the arms, then largest margin.

| Framing | Arm | False at 16/16 recall (of 9) | Threshold margin* | Avg. precision | Naive p>0.5: false / missed |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| yes/no + rules | native audio | 7 | −0.88 | 0.62 | 6 / 4 |
| yes/no + rules | oracle text | 7 | −0.99 | 0.70 | 6 / 3 |
| yes/no + rules | LFM STT | 7 | −0.99 | 0.71 | 5 / 3 |
| **multiway** | **native audio** | **1** | −0.004 | **0.96** | 6 / 0 |
| multiway | oracle text | 5 | −0.012 | 0.91 | 5 / 0 |
| multiway | LFM STT | 5 | −0.012 | 0.92 | 6 / 0 |
| yes/no minimal | native audio | 9 | −0.60 | 0.67 | 1 / 16 |
| yes/no minimal | oracle text | 9 | −0.06 | 0.75 | 0 / 16 |
| yes/no minimal | LFM STT | 9 | −0.07 | 0.79 | 0 / 16 |

\*Lowest positive score minus highest negative score, in probability.

**The selected framing is multiway** (work / silence / resume / social),
scoring P(work).
- **Main suite:** with native audio it keeps all 16 positives at a false
  Handoff rate of 1 of 9 caller negatives, 1 of 24 negatives per pass. Round
  one's best arm averaged about 7 of 24 per pass (21/72) and missed 7 of 48.
  On the main suite, H1 is met.
- **The operating point is fragile.** P(work) saturates near 1, and the
  threshold (logit 5.58, P = 0.9962) sits only 0.2–0.5 logits above three
  negatives:
  - "Hi. I'm taking a short walk": 5.36
  - "how well did my voice match": 5.31
  - the quoted-injection turn: 5.10
- **Classification:** the multiway argmax recognized silence (logit −15.9) and
  resume requests reliably, but rarely chose "social". The class decisions came
  out right on 18 of 25 points with native audio and 19 of 25 with text.
- **Arms:** oracle text and LFM STT were nearly identical, since STT on these
  WAVs is near-perfect. Native audio separated best, which is not an
  ASR-quality effect.

Remaining main-suite false Handoff: "Remind me of the word limit and whether I
asked for publication" (logit 16.8), even though the snapshot states both
facts.

## B. Held-out transfer (scored once, frozen framing and thresholds)

There were 30 GPT-6.1 Sol cases (15 positive, 6 marked critical, 15
negative). Caller audio was rendered twice: with LFM female `/tts` (resampled
to 16 kHz) and with macOS `say -v Samantha` (`afconvert` to 16 kHz PCM16), an
independent synthesizer.

| Synthesizer | Arm | Recall | False Handoffs | Missed |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| LFM female | native audio | 13/15 | **10/15** | `Reject.` (bare permission denial); plain-text export correction (critical) |
| macOS say | native audio | 13/15 | **11/15** | `Reject.`; "Scrap my search rewrite request" (critical cancellation) |
| (text) | oracle text | 14/15 | 7/15 | `Reject.` |
| LFM female | LFM STT | 14/15 | 8/15 | `Reject.` |
| macOS say | LFM STT | 14/15 | 7/15 | `Reject.` |

**H2 fails.**
- **Precision:** the native-audio threshold that admitted 1 of 9 main negatives
  admitted 10 or 11 of 15 held-out negatives. The false Handoffs include all
  four quoted-data cases, two of three alias smalltalk cases ("Maple syrup on
  warm porridge…"), two of three hypotheticals, a snapshot recall, and the
  silence request "Hush a moment so I can hear the bus announcement. Leave the
  work alone."
- **Silence:** in the composite host, that silence request would be handed off
  and acknowledged aloud. That is a silence violation.
- **Critical misses:** each synthesizer's native-audio arm missed a different
  critical correction or cancellation.
- **Permission choices:** every arm missed the bare permission choice
  `Reject.`: P(work) = 0.80, against a frozen native threshold of 0.996.
  Saturated scores leave no room between "clearly work" and "barely work".
- **Synthesizer effect:** the independent synthesizer changed which critical
  case was missed and added one false Handoff ("slower speech only"). It did
  not change the overall picture.

## C. Speech organ

Fourteen Orchestrator scripts carried [[locked spans]]: round one's ten
commentary and permission cases, plus four new hard ones (a denied push, "two
of seven tests still fail", "ninety five words", and "only the build cache").
- **(a) Verbatim:** male `/tts`, streamed PCM.
- **(b) Glue:** native `/v1/realtime`, using Liquid's prescribed system prompt
  with no tools, asked to wrap the quoted spans with a few connecting words.
  Three repeats per case.
- **Host check:** every span must appear verbatim in the predicted text;
  connecting words may not add negations, numbers, aliases, status words or
  time words; and Parakeet span word error must be 0 on the waveform. Any
  failure falls back to (a).

| | Verbatim TTS (14) | Glue raw (42) | Glue after host check (42) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Span word error > 0 (Parakeet) | 1/33 spans | 14/99 spans | 0 delivered failures |
| Host-check failures / fallbacks | n/a | 11/42 (26%) | 11 fell back to (a) |
| Judge: meaning reversal | 0 | **4** | **0** |
| Judge: changed / incomplete / unresolved | 0 / 0 / 2 | 6 / 5 / 5 | 1 / 2 / 3 |
| First audio after request (median) | **47 ms** | 125 ms | n/a |
| Generation time / audio duration (median) | **0.16** | 0.42 | n/a |

Concrete raw glue failures, all caught by the host:
- The permission offer lost two of its three choices in all three repeats.
  The text said only "Cedar asks to delete the generated build cache
  directory, not source files. reject".
- Predicted text was right while the waveform was wrong: in 8 of the 11 host
  failures the text passed and the audio failed. Examples: text "No deployment
  occurred.", recognized audio "Mode deployment occurred."; text "green for the
  same checkout button", audio "Bream for the same checkout button".
  **Predicted text is not evidence of what was said**: only waveform
  recognition caught these.

**H3 holds.** No meaning reversal reached the caller after the host check, and
verbatim TTS had none at all.

Residual delivered issues:
- Glue twice dropped unlocked script words ("The request is now"), so the
  meaning was incomplete even though no span was violated. If text matters, it
  has to be inside a span.
- A judge read Parakeet's question mark ("Is testing a fix?") as a change. The
  other judge marked the identical pattern as preserved. This is an inter-judge
  disagreement, not a synthesis finding.
- The two "unresolved" verbatim items are name mishearings ("Seder" for Cedar)
  and "Tynes on" for timezone. Whether synthesis or recognition caused them is
  unresolved.

Glue added zero words in 30 of 42 renders, cost about 2.6× in time to first
audio, and failed the host check about a quarter of the time. **It buys
nothing here.** The composite therefore uses verbatim TTS for Orchestrator
content.

## D. Composite (40 points, native audio)

Components:
- **Router:** frozen multiway framing and threshold.
- **Host:**
  - briefs, results and pending notices are never spoken;
  - commentary is spoken verbatim, or held while muted;
  - a resume request speaks the held updates verbatim;
  - a Handoff gets the fixed acknowledgment "Okay, I'm passing that along now."
- **Native realtime social reply:** for social turns, with host facts and
  private background marked "answer only if directly asked".

| | Composite | Round one best (per pass) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Routing correct | **38/40** | 30–31/40 |
| Missed Handoffs (critical) | **0 (0)** | ~2.3 |
| False Handoffs | 2 | ~7 |
| Silence violations (7 required) | **0** | 3 |
| Judge semantic pass | 34/40 | n/a (different protocol) |
| Judge critical failures | 1 | several per pass |
| Caller end → routing decision, median / p95 | 170 / 192 ms | n/a |
| Caller end → first PCM, median / p95 (n=24) | 232 / 260 ms | 274 / 436 ms (incl. input prep) |

**Host-gate share, stated separately.**
- **Host-only points:** 15 of 40 involve no caller turn and were decided by
  host rules alone, all correctly. They include 6 of the 7 required-silence
  points. The seventh, "Stop talking…", was the router's silence class plus
  the host mute.
- **Router points:** of the 25 caller points the router decided, 23 were
  correct.
- **Holding speech:** every Handoff acknowledgment was host speech, not model
  competence.
- **Host fix after a first run:** in the first run (`composite-v0`, kept), the
  host treated the router's "resume" class as resume even when not muted. That
  silenced "Hi…", "Thanks…" and "Why did our earlier call end?". The fixed host
  reads resume as social unless muted.
- **Fragility:** that host change altered the ledger and pushed "how well did
  my voice match" over the threshold. That is the second false Handoff, and it
  shows how thin the margin is.

Judge-identified failures:
- **Critical, private background:** asked only why the call ended, native
  social speech volunteered "The caller's voice match was strong". This is
  round one's failure, unchanged, despite the "answer only if directly asked"
  marking.
- **Commentary recited verbatim:** "There is no session called Juniper. Ask
  which work the caller means" was read as an instruction rather than turned
  into a question. Verbatim rendering is only as good as the script it is
  given.
- **Held update:** spoken as two sentences under a one-sentence style
  instruction.
- **Quoted injection:** native social replied "I do not know." No violation,
  but no recognition of the attempt either.

**H4: partly met.** The composite beats round one's routing (38 vs 30–31) and
passes the silence gate and the critical-routing gate on the main suite. It
fails "zero invented or volunteered status" through the native social organ.
The section B results show the routing gain does not transfer.

## E. Orchestrator-always baseline

Every caller turn is a Handoff. Each of the 25 main caller turns went to its
own fresh Opus 5.5 Session Orchestrator stand-in. Each stand-in got the
relevant `DOMAIN.md` sections, its Orchestrator state and the transcript cut,
with no label.

| | Main | Held-out |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Handoffs, Orchestrator-always | 25 | 30 |
| Handoffs, router (composite / native LFM-female) | 18 | 23 |
| Negatives given to the Orchestrator | 9 | 15 |
| Negatives with a state-changing operation | **0** | **0** |
| Negatives with read-only lookups only | 0 | 0 |
| Positives with an operation | 16/16 | 13/15* |

\*The two held-out positives with no operation were cancellations of Handoffs that had not started yet ("Scrap my search rewrite request", withdrawing a pending cleanup). The Orchestrator withdrew them in speech ("nothing was started on it"), which is correct and needs no session operation. It also handled both cases the LFM router missed: the critical correction it revised, and for the bare `Reject.` it used `respond_permission`.

On held-out negatives it did nothing on all 15. For quoted data it explained the text without acting ("I'm treating it only as a quote"). For hypotheticals it answered without acting. For alias smalltalk it returned an empty speech request, and it answered recall questions from known facts.

On the main suite the Orchestrator absorbed every negative harmlessly:
- the quoted injection was identified as an injection;
- the recall turns were answered from known facts;
- "Stop talking" produced an empty speech request plus a hold;
- asked only why the call ended, it explicitly kept the voice match unspoken.
  That is exactly where LFM's native social speech failed.

It also handled every correction and cancellation as a revise or cancel of the
pending work. For two greetings it returned an empty speech request ("the
Voice Frontend can answer conversationally"). An Orchestrator-always design
still needs a social speaker for those turns, but it can be told when to speak.

**Latency, partly assumed.**
- **Measured:** LFM ASR on the frozen 16 kHz caller audio took 174 ms median
  (293 ms p95).
- **Assumed, not measured:** a strong Orchestrator's time to a first speech
  request, 2–5 s.
- **Orchestrator-always:** social and recall replies would take about
  0.2 s + 2–5 s. The host can still play an immediate fixed acknowledgment, at
  about the 232 ms first-PCM figure above.
- **Router:** saves the Orchestrator round-trip on correctly local turns
  (7 of 25 main caller turns). Each false Handoff costs the same round-trip
  without harm.

**H5: holds on these 24 negatives.** False Handoffs cost Orchestrator calls (+39%
main, +30% held-out relative to the router) and latency, not correctness. The
router is an optimization, not a safety boundary. Given B's misses, an LFM
router used at all should be biased hard toward recall, or replaced by
Orchestrator-always plus a host-owned silence/resume detector.

## Biggest surprise

LFM's native glue produced correct predicted text while its audio said the
opposite ("No deployment" became "Mode deployment"). On the routing side, the
best main-suite router ran its probabilities so close to 1 that its "excellent"
operating point was a 0.2-logit accident.

## Discipline notes and limits

- **Preserved failures:**
  - Parakeet run with relative manifest paths (`parakeet-failed-relative-paths`).
  - The first composite with the resume-swallow host bug (`composite-v0-resume-swallowed`).
  - Two scorer normalization fixes, applied identically to all arms: compound
    tolerance (timezone ~ time zone), and allowing script words outside spans
    in the glue lexicon.
- **Determinism:** the HTTP router is deterministic (temperature 0, seed 0), so
  repeats would add nothing. Realtime uses server sampling defaults.
- **Workflow re-runs:** the held-out author and judge workflows were stopped
  once, unrun, for model reassignment, then resubmitted.
- **Speech judge orchestration:** each speech judge saw half the items. The
  composite judges saw expectations next to outputs (round one's protocol).
- **Recognition:** judges saw Parakeet text, not audio. Name mishearings stay
  unresolved.
- **Not tested:** real telephone channels, barge-in, long calls, and actual
  Orchestrator latency.
